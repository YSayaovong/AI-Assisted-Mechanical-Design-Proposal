# Gen AI Use Case: AI-Augmented Mechanical Design for Manufacturing

**Role:** Mechanical Designer — Industrial Manufacturing
**Period:** H1 2025 (Research & Development)
**Tools:** Claude · ChatGPT · Microsoft Copilot (M365) · Perplexity AI · AutoCAD · SolidWorks · ERP System
**Deliverable:** Executive Pitch Deck · Business Case · 90-Day Pilot Proposal

---

## Project Background

As a mechanical designer operating inside a manufacturing environment, I identified a systemic inefficiency in the design workflow: engineers were spending the majority of their time on tasks that did not require engineering judgment — researching standards, drafting routine notes, writing ECO descriptions, and cycling through preventable rework loops.

This project applies a data analyst lens to that problem. Rather than simply documenting the pain, I quantified the time loss, modeled the cost impact, and designed a Gen AI-augmented workflow as the proposed solution — then built the business case to support organizational adoption.

The project answers four operational questions:

- **Where is engineering time being lost in the design cycle?**
- **What is the measurable cost of rework and revision inefficiency?**
- **How can Gen AI reduce cycle time without compromising engineering quality?**
- **What does a viable, low-risk pilot program look like?**

Insights and recommendations are structured around four key areas:

- **Process Inefficiency Analysis:** Identifying where routine tasks consume disproportionate designer time
- **Rework Cost Impact:** Quantifying the financial burden of ECO rework loops on operational margins
- **AI Workflow Design:** Mapping Gen AI tools to specific steps in the design process
- **ROI & Pilot Proposal:** Building a 3-year business case and 90-day implementation roadmap

---

## Problem Statement

Mechanical designers in manufacturing environments face four compounding inefficiencies that prevent them from spending time on actual engineering work:

| Pain Point | Description | Estimated Time Lost |
|---|---|---|
| Repetitive Drawing Revisions | Routine ECOs — title block updates, BOM entries, standard note revisions | 40–50% of design cycle |
| Specification Research Overload | Manual lookup of industry standards, material datasheets, and internal guides | 4+ hrs per design job |
| Rework from Miscommunication | Ambiguous drawing notes and verbal change requests create loops between Design, Manufacturing, and QA | 3–4 rework loops per order |
| Limited Design Variant Exploration | Time pressure forces commitment to first viable design, leaving cost and performance improvements untapped | 1 option reviewed vs. 3+ possible |

> **Core finding:** The design process is not engineering-constrained — it is process-constrained. Designers are capable of higher-value output; the workflow prevents it. Gen AI directly addresses the constraint without replacing engineering judgment.

## Proposed Solution: Gen AI Workflow Stack

A purpose-built Gen AI toolkit layered into the existing mechanical design workflow — no CAD replacement, no system overhaul.

| Tool | Role in Workflow | Key Capabilities |
|---|---|---|
| Claude / ChatGPT | Spec Research & Documentation | Instant standard lookups, drawing note drafts, design intent explanations |
| Copilot (M365) | Drawing & BOM Workflow | Auto-populates title blocks, drafts ECO change descriptions, summarizes change requests |
| Perplexity AI | Standards & Vendor Research | Real-time industry standard lookup with cited sources, supplier spec benchmarking |
| ChatGPT / Claude | Design Variant Analysis | Generates parametric design options, compares material trade-offs, surfaces similar past designs |

## AI-Augmented Workflow: Step-by-Step

The redesigned workflow moves a designer from order intake to completed drawing package in approximately 2.5 hours — versus 1–2 full days under the traditional process.

| Step | Activity | AI Role | Time (AI-Assisted) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Receive & Parse the Order | Extracts key parameters, flags non-standard requirements | ~15 min |
| 2 | Research Applicable Standards | Surfaces relevant standard clauses and clearance requirements | ~20 min |
| 3 | Generate Design Notes & BOM | Drafts component specs, material notes, and BOM line descriptions | ~45 min |
| 4 | Explore Design Variants | Generates structured trade-off comparison across 2–3 configurations | ~30 min |
| 5 | Draft ECO & Drawing Package | Drafts ECO change description, cross-checks BOM consistency | ~40 min |

**Total time with AI: ~2.5 hrs · Traditional approach: 1–2 full days · Time savings: 60–70%**

## Open Questions & Risk Mitigation

| Consideration | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| IP & Drawing Confidentiality | Proprietary designs entered into public AI tools | Evaluate enterprise-tier options (Copilot, Azure OpenAI) with data residency controls |
| Accuracy of AI-Generated Specs | AI output released without engineering review | Mandatory human-review gate — AI drafts, engineer approves before release |
| CAD System Integration | Direct AI-to-CAD integration limited today | Near-term: AI assists outside CAD. Medium-term: API-based title block and BOM automation |
| Designer Adoption & Trust | Skepticism of AI-generated specs | Structured onboarding, transparent AI output labeling, early wins on low-risk tasks |

## 90-Day Pilot Proposal

| Phase | Timeline | Activities |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | Days 1–30 | Tool selection, IP/data policy review, designer onboarding |
| Phase 2 | Days 31–60 | Pilot: Apply AI to one live design order end-to-end |
| Phase 3 | Days 61–90 | Measure cycle time, rework rate, and quality — present findings to leadership |

The pilot is designed to be low-risk, low-cost, and fully reversible. It requires no CAD system changes, no infrastructure investment, and no disruption to active production workflows outside the single pilot order.

---

## Recommendations

**1. Begin with Copilot (M365) as the entry point.** It integrates directly into the existing Microsoft environment, requires no new vendor approval in most organizations, and delivers immediate value on ECO drafts and drawing note generation — the two highest-frequency routine tasks.

**2. Establish an AI output review protocol before piloting.** Any AI-generated spec, note, or BOM entry must pass through a named engineer for approval before it enters a drawing. Documenting this process before the pilot begins removes the accuracy risk and builds designer trust simultaneously.

**3. Track the right metrics from day one.** Cycle time per drawing package, rework loops per order, and spec research time per job should be baselined in the two weeks before the pilot begins. Without a clean baseline, the ROI case cannot be made with confidence to leadership.

**4. Target Error Correction ECOs for the first AI-assisted workflow.** These are high-frequency, high-stakes change events where drawing note quality directly affects whether a correction actually holds. Using AI to draft ECO descriptions for correction events — with mandatory engineer review — addresses the highest-risk revision type first.

**5. Plan for Phase 4 beyond the pilot.** If the 90-day pilot validates the time savings, the natural extension is BOM automation via CAD API integration. Beginning the technical evaluation of AutoCAD and SolidWorks API capabilities during Phase 3 ensures continuity without delay.
